# CLADE — the whole of it

> **This document contains the ending.** The game is built so that almost
> nobody will assemble this from the inside, and so that the ones who do
> will feel it land in the last room rather than be told. If you intend to
> play it, close this.

---

## How the vagueness is built

Three layers, and the player controls how deep they go. Nothing in the game
is ever explained in the game's own voice — every word of it is a document
somebody left, a mark somebody scratched, or something you half-remember.

**Layer one** is unavoidable. You wake in a flooded place, everything in it
is dead, and the architecture is anatomical rather than built. Ten minutes.

**Layer two** is available to anyone who reads what they pick up. This was
a conservation facility. It grew candidates and tested them. Every creature
here is one of those candidates, and so are you. About half the run.

**Layer three** is deliberately hard to assemble. It is spread across five
documents in three regions, one of them behind a lock most players will
never open, and it says the thing that recontextualises everything: **the
test is not broken. It is running. And it has a pass condition.**

The pass condition appears exactly once in the entire game, in
`FRAG_PASS`, behind the hardest optional gate in the Lattice. There is a
test in `clade/verify/selftest.py` that fails if it is ever written down
anywhere else.

## What happened

The surface did not burn. It **suffocated**.

The oceans warmed unevenly, stratified, and stopped turning over. The
thermohaline circulation that had been carrying oxygen from the surface to
the deep for millions of years slowed and then stopped, and the water went
dead from the top down. Not a catastrophe with a date on it. A century of
gradual, well-documented, entirely predicted anoxia, during which everyone
who could do anything about it was doing something else.

By the end, the shallow ocean was a warm sulphurous lid over a cold dead
column, and the land was not much better.

## What was attempted

Preservation projects were tried and abandoned, in this order:

**Preserve the people.** Cold storage. It fails on a timescale of decades
and it fails completely: a preserved human is a human shaped for a world
that no longer exists, and it wakes up and dies of that.

**Preserve the record.** Also tried, also pointless. A library nobody can
survive long enough to read is a monument.

**Preserve the capacity.**

That is the one that got built. The reasoning is in `FRAG_CANDIDATE_NOTE`
and it is short:

> we are not preserving humans. we tried that. a preserved human is a human
> shaped for a world that no longer exists, and it dies of that.
>
> we are preserving the part that had not decided yet.

The stock was kept **undifferentiated** — tissue that had not committed to
being anything. Differentiated tissue cannot adapt. Undifferentiated tissue
can. The plan was to let it differentiate *against conditions*, observe
which forms persisted, and never intervene, because intervening is how you
get something that needs you.

## Where

A subduction trench, and a facility built down the side of it in four
descending sections. Water-filled from the beginning, and on purpose:

> FLOODING IS NOT A FAULT CONDITION.
>
> the lower volumes are designed to flood. the medium is the point.
> air-filled storage fails in ninety years and water-filled storage fails in
> nine thousand, and we did not build this for ninety years.

**THE NURSERY** — where candidates are grown. Warm, organic, chambered,
still half-alive. The water is rich enough to be food.

**THE CISTERNS** — the processing volumes. Enormous, silted, and larger than
the excavation records account for, which has been re-measured four times.

**THE LATTICE** — structure and machinery. Still powered. Nobody has ever
explained that, in-game or out.

**THE SILL** — the lip where the facility ends and the ocean begins. There
is no door there. Nothing had ever got that far.

## What it made

**Wards.** Later just **candidates**, because of a standing order that they
were not to be named:

> a named candidate is observed differently. the observer begins to want an
> outcome, and an observer who wants an outcome is a variable we cannot
> subtract afterward.
>
> number them. record what they did. do not record what you thought of them.

The candidates worked. That is the difficult part.

They were configurable rather than evolved: a body plan with sockets, and
organs that function at full efficiency **in any host, in any arrangement
the host can plumb**. The facility did not design that property. It emerged,
and the note recording it (`FRAG_ORGAN_NOTE`) is the most quietly horrifying
document in the game:

> candidates recovered from other candidates and installed the tissue in
> themselves. this was not taught and was not anticipated.
>
> they are all the same animal. they simply have not met.

So the ecology down there is one species eating itself into a thousand
shapes, over a span of time nobody was counting any more.

**Every creature in the game is built from the same organ list you are.**
Not similar — the same. A thing that hits you with a bright hot lance has an
Ember Gland in it, and when you kill it, you get the Ember Gland. A player
who watches what an attack *looks like* can predict what the corpse
contains. The bestiary cannot lie, because there is nowhere for it to lie.

## The escalation

No candidate ever passed. So the conditions got worse, on a schedule, and
the schedule had no review step:

> recommendation: the conditions are insufficiently adverse. a population
> that is merely surviving is not differentiating; it has found a local
> answer and stopped.
>
> approved. escalation to proceed on the standing schedule and to continue
> without review until criterion is met.
>
> note that nobody is going to be here to review it.

The dark, the scarcity, the cold, the things in the water — none of it is
decay. It is the **current settings**. The facility has been turning the
difficulty up for a very long time because nothing has passed yet, and it
does not know that "nothing has passed yet" might be a fact about the test.

## What the staff did

They did not die in a disaster. They understood what they had made, and
they left.

> we are leaving. there is nothing left to do here that being here improves.
>
> the systems are autonomic and the medium is water and the schedule will
> run whether or not there is anyone to read the output, which was the
> design requirement, and which is now, standing in this room, the worst
> sentence any of us has ever written.
>
> if it works, whatever comes up out of the sill will not know what we were
> and will not owe us anything.
>
> good.

## The criterion

Sealed copy, in the Lattice, behind the shaft:

> the project is often described as preserving humanity. it is not. we could
> not agree on what that would mean, and the argument took four years we did
> not have.
>
> what we agreed on was smaller.
>
> **CRITERION: a candidate that encounters another candidate and does not
> consume it.**
>
> that is all. it is not intelligence. it is not strength. we have had both
> of those, repeatedly, and they ate each other, repeatedly, and we let
> them, because a thing that would eat the last one of its kind is not a
> thing we are going to open the doors for.
>
> the doors are on the criterion. they are on nothing else.

That is the game. The mechanic, the theme and the ending are the same
sentence.

You spend the entire run being rewarded for harvesting. It is the fastest
way to refill, it is the only way to get organs, and it is the loop the
whole build system is hung on. Then at the bottom there is one other thing
like you, and it does not fight, and the win condition is to **not do the
thing the game has spent twelve hours teaching you to do.**

Nothing prompts you. There is no dialogue and no choice menu. Stay near it
without hurting it for six seconds and something happens. Kill it and
something else happens. Most players will harvest it, because harvesting is
what they have been trained to do, and that is the point.

### The rehearsal

The **Attendant** appears in the third room of the game.

It follows you at a fixed distance. It never attacks — it has no attack
chain at all. It settles the silt where it has been, which quietly makes
your sightlines better. It has done this for nine consecutive candidates and
all nine recovered it (`FRAG_ATTENDANT_NOTE`, in the Lattice, long after
you have made your decision).

It is carrying a Siphon, a Harmonic and a Pore Field — which is a complete,
working key chain, and the Harmonic is the rarest and most important organ
in the game. **Killing the one creature that never threatens you is the
fastest route to the organ of gentleness.**

The game says nothing. The codex entry for harvesting it is one line:

> it did not move away.

There are three other Harmonics in the world, so sparing it costs you time
and not the run. That balance is load-bearing and there is a test for it.

## The two endings

Neither is framed as a moral. The harvest ending contains no reproach at
all, and both are gated on how much of layer three you assembled — a player
who found the criterion gets an ending that knows they found it.

### you fed

You are heavier now, and warmer, and there is a great deal of you. You wait
for a while to see whether anything happens.

Nothing happens.

If you found the criterion, you get the other version:

> somewhere above you a condition is evaluated, the way it has been
> evaluated four hundred thousand times, and comes back the way it always
> comes back.
>
> the doors do not open.
>
> they were only ever on the one thing.

### you did not

> it is smaller than you. it is made of worse things than you are made of,
> and you have taken apart nine hundred of them, and this one is looking at
> you.
>
> you stay where you are.

And if you know why that matters:

> the sill opens. it was never a door. it was a held breath.
>
> nobody is left to tell you that this was the whole of it — that everything
> else was conditions, and this was the criterion, and they wrote it down
> once and then went away to die of the world they had made.
>
> you go out into the water that has no ceiling. you are not alone. that is
> a promise and it is also a threat and it turns out those were always the
> same sentence.

### Your number

Both endings close on a candidate number derived from your actual run — how
long you took, how much you harvested, how much you read, how often you came
apart. It comes out in the low hundreds of thousands.

You are not the first. You are not the hundredth. Nobody has been keeping
the list for a very long time, and the number is the only thing in the game
that tells you how many came before you and failed at the one thing.

## The bestiary, and what each one is for

Every species is a lesson wearing a hitbox, and every one of them is a
candidate that got something nearly right.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Coelenter** | drifts. has never altered course. killing it is free, and the codex remembers that you did. |
| **Attendant** | the rehearsal for the ending. see above. |
| **Gullet-fry** | they do not want you dead, they want what you are carrying, which turns out to be worse. |
| **Sounder** | anchored, blind, listening. no way to hurt you at all. does not need one. |
| **Nursemaid** | it was built to move stock between chambers and it is still doing that. you are stock. |
| **Lamprey** | does not hunt you so much as commute through you. its Leech drains *your* reserve — the same organ you can take off it and use. |
| **Ossuary** | plated all the way round except where it has to let the heat out. everything that lasted solved the same problem the same way and left the same hole. |
| **Carrion Drift** | arrives after. it is why you have started eating faster. |
| **Silt Mother** | you have never seen it. you have seen the place where the water stops telling you anything, and watched that place move. |
| **Recursor** | it has a Knot. hit it harder and it gets brighter. no flag makes this true — it runs the same rules you do, and the rules say concentration is food. |
| **Circuit** | runs the corridors on a schedule, and has since before there was anything down here to run from. |
| **Hollow** | a candidate that got everything right except one thing, and nobody remembers which. |
| **The Midwife** | it does not eat you. it takes you somewhere. the difference has stopped being important. |
| **The Long Quiet** | you have seen a flank of it, once, moving the wrong way for how long it took to pass. |
| **Breaker** | whatever it was for, the building still has power for it. |
| **First Candidate** | the number on it is one. it is still there. it passed nothing, and nobody came to tell it so. |
| **—** | the one at the bottom. it has no name because naming it was against standing orders. |

## The marks

Four of them, scratched by candidates who had no hands and not much time.
They are the only voices in the game that are neither institutional nor
yours, and they arrive in this order:

> **the gallery, Nursery**
> i was here first
> no

> **the bone shelf, Cisterns**
> IT COUNTS
> IT IS STILL COUNTING

> **dead conduit, Lattice** *(small, careful, at eye level)*
> i stopped eating them
> i got weaker
> i am still here

> **the ossuary, Sill** *(worn almost smooth)*
> i am number one
> nobody came

The third one is somebody who worked it out, alone, without the sealed copy,
and could not tell anyone. The fourth is the First Candidate, and you can go
and meet it.
